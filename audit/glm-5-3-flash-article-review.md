## Review

## (a) Summary

The report asks whether SMART attributes read daily by `smartctl` can estimate the probability and likely timing of hard-drive failures, using Backblaze's Drive Stats panel (2016–2025, 646,777,037 rows, 478,464 drives). It formulates failure prediction as a discrete-time survival model (LightGBM on person-period rows, right-censoring, inverse-probability sampling weights tempered to the power 0.5 with per-bin Platt recalibration), evaluated on drive- and time-disjoint test data (2024–2025) with ablations for label circularity (removing counters 5/187/188/197/198), usage-only features, weight-power sensitivity, and a dedicated 30-day classifier, plus drive-level bootstrap intervals. It finds SMART raises 30-day ROC-AUC from 0.604 to 0.876 (~62% of the gain persists without the operator's replacement counters), the model beats a counter rule under fixed false-alarm budgets (77% vs 59% of failures alerted at a 1% budget, precision 4.4%), silent failures (~21%) remain barely predictable, remaining-life estimates are pessimistic, and probabilities drift in 2025 — concluding SMART is useful for short-horizon triage, not for timing failures months ahead.
