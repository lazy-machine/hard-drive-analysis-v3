# Validity of the Backblaze Drive Stats dataset for general conclusions about SMART-based failure prediction

Scope: evidence for and against using Backblaze's public Drive Stats data (the project uses 2016-01-01 to 2025-12-31, HDDs only, boot SSDs excluded, drive model and vendor as features) to make general claims about whether `smartctl` (SMART) readings predict failure.

Stance labels are relative to the project's methodology:
- **[in favor]**: supports using Backblaze data for this purpose.
- **[against]**: challenges representativeness, label quality or data quality.
- **[mixed]**: has both kinds of evidence, or supports one aspect and challenges another.

Source-type flags: **[primary: Backblaze]** is the dataset owner's own documentation or blog, **[peer-reviewed]**, **[preprint]**, **[journalism]**, **[community]** (forums, GitHub, blogs; low weight).

---

## Q1. How does Backblaze define a "failure", has the definition changed, and what happens to drives removed for other reasons (censoring)?

### Takeaway
A Backblaze "failure" is an operational removal label, not a physical failure-mode label. It has been described three ways over time: removal-and-replacement (2014), disappearance from the daily population (2016 to 2026), and an automated query against Jira work tickets with a quarter-end lookback (made explicit in 2025). Backblaze partly decides to remove a drive by looking at SMART 5/187/188/197/198, so the label is partly a product of the features the project wants to test. Drives removed for upgrades or migrations are meant to drop out without being flagged (right-censoring), but Backblaze itself documents cases where temporary removals were counted as failures.

### Cited Findings
- **[primary: Backblaze] [against: label circularity]** Brian Beach, "Hard Drive SMART Stats," Backblaze Blog, 12 Nov 2014. Definition: "Backblaze counts a drive as failed when it is removed from a Storage Pod and replaced" because it has "totally stopped working" or has "shown evidence of failing soon." The same post says: "Backblaze uses SMART 5, 187, 188, 197, and 198 for determining the failure or potential failure of a hard drive." — [Backblaze](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/hard-drive-smart-stats/)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [mixed]** Andy Klein, "What SMART Stats Tell Us About Hard Drives," Backblaze Blog, 6 Oct 2016. 76.7% of failed drives had at least one of the five SMART stats above zero, and 23.3% showed no warning in any of them. 4.2% of operational drives also had one or more of the five above zero. Failed drives "are marked as such and their data is no longer logged." Drives removed for other reasons, such as Storage Pod upgrades, are not marked as failures. A raw value above zero is "a reason to investigate": Backblaze also looks at RAID array status, Vault status and internal logs, and a drive with several correlated errors "should be scheduled for replacement." So SMART feeds a human-judgment removal decision; it is not an automatic trigger. — [Backblaze](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/what-smart-stats-indicate-hard-drive-failures/)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [against: label noise]** "Backblaze Drive Stats for Q3 2025," Backblaze Blog, Nov 2025. The failure definition is "something we haven't addressed since the early days of this series," and the process changed from manual to automated. Base rule: "A failure in Drive Stats occurs when a drive vanishes out of the reporting population. It is considered 'failed' until it shows up again." Rules: a drive that logs data on the last day of the quarter has not failed; a drive that is the primary serial in a drive-replacement Jira ticket has failed; a drive that is the target of a clone or temporary-replacement ticket has not failed; three human-curated tables are cross-referenced. Backblaze admits "cosmetic" failures when work on a model lasts longer than the quarterly collection window. Example: Toshiba MG08ACA16TEY (16 TB) showed a 16.95% quarterly AFR because drives were pulled for a firmware/upgrade project; Backblaze says "the failure wasn't mechanical." — [Backblaze](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q3-2025/); press summary: [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251111306009/en/Backblaze-Q3-2025-Drive-Stats-Rethinking-Failure-Celebrating-High-Capacity-Drive-Strength)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [against: label noise and missing early failures]** Stephanie Doyle, "Backblaze Drive Stats for Q1 2026," Backblaze Blog, 9 Jul 2026. A C++ program logs the failure: "If a drive was there the day before and is not there the day of, then it's logged as a failure." A drive that reappears on day two or later is retroactively not counted (a "lookback period"), but the lookback stops at quarter end, so "there's a handful of drives that might be false positives." Drives that fail on their first day are never in the data: "we can't define day one failures using only the Drive Stats program." Asked whether these are under-reported, the answer is "Short answer: yes, probably," though Backblaze calls them rare because drives are qualified before deployment. Unusual Q1 2026 data led the team to "revisit and validate the assumptions behind our Drive Stats analysis." — [Backblaze](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q1-2026/)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [mixed]** The official data page says Backblaze takes "a snapshot of each operational drive" every day, one row per active drive, and warns that "schema changes from quarter to quarter do occur." The page does not define failure. — [Backblaze Hard Drive Test Data](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/resources/hard-drive-test-data)
- **[peer-reviewed] [mixed: label type is common across the field]** Industry labels are also replacement labels. Lu et al. (FAST '20) "consider a disk to be failed when the production data center operator deems a disk necessary to be replaced," which their operator does after a failed read/write that persists after restart. — [USENIX FAST '20 paper PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast20-lu.pdf). Schroeder and Gibson (FAST '07) also study "field replacement" rates as a proxy for failure. — [USENIX FAST '07](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast-07/disk-failures-real-world-what-does-mttf-1000000-hours-mean-you)

### Inferences
- **Circularity risk.** If Backblaze staff pull drives partly because SMART 5/187/188/197/198 are non-zero, a model trained on Backblaze labels learns, in part, Backblaze's replacement policy. Predictive power for those attributes is therefore partly built in. Claims of the form "SMART predicts failure" should be framed as "SMART predicts Backblaze's removal decisions." Ablations without those five attributes, or measuring lead time beyond when the attributes first go non-zero, would test this.
- **Censoring.** Planned removals (upgrades, migrations, decommissioning) are supposed to show as a serial that stops reporting with `failure=0`. That is right-censoring, and a survival or hazard formulation should treat it that way, not as "healthy until the end."
- **Label-noise bursts tied to operations.** The Q3 2025 Toshiba case shows that an operations project can create a burst of `failure=1` labels concentrated in one model. Because the project uses drive model as a feature, such bursts can inflate model-level hazards and the apparent value of the model feature.
- **Definition drift.** Over 2016 to 2025 the labeling went from manual to automated and Jira-assisted. The effective definition may not be constant across the study window.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed: whether the `failure` column in the *public daily CSVs* comes from the same Jira-curated quarterly logic as the blog reports, or only from the "missing next day" C++ rule. Also not confirmed whether past daily files are retroactively corrected when a drive reappears. No Backblaze document found that states this.
- No date found for when the process became automated, so the definition drift cannot be placed inside 2016 to 2025.
- No count found of how many removals per year are planned migrations versus failures.

## Q2. Is the Backblaze fleet representative? Critiques (consumer vs enterprise, single operator, environment, model mix, small per-model samples) and Backblaze's responses

### Takeaway
The best-sourced critiques focus on small per-model and enterprise samples, results skewed by a few problem models, and the fact that it is one operator with one chassis design and workload. Most of the detailed critiques date from 2014 to 2015, when the fleet was small and mostly consumer drives. The fleet and the public schema have since grown (data centers, clusters, slot positions), which reduces some concerns. But it is still one company's archival-storage workload, and the drive model mix reflects what Backblaze chose to buy.

### Cited Findings
- **[journalism] [against]** Dan Olds, "How NOT to evaluate hard disk reliability: Backblaze vs world+dog," *The Register*, 17 Feb 2014. The enterprise comparison rests on 368 drive-years against 14,719 consumer drive-years: "This is a damned small sample on the data centre drive side." Two more surviving enterprise drives would have reversed the 4.2% vs 4.6% result. "Backblaze's Seagate results are hugely skewed by two drive models." Olds also raises the question of whether consumer drives were used under suitable traffic, and concludes "the foundations underlying the Backblaze conclusions aren't all that sturdy." Olds credits Henry Newman's Enterprise Storage Forum analysis, which I could not retrieve. — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2014/02/17/backblaze_how_not_to_evaluate_disk_reliability/)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [mixed]** Brian Beach, "Enterprise Drives: Fact or Fiction?," Backblaze Blog, 2013. Enterprise drives: 17 failures in 368 drive-years (4.6% AFR); consumer drives: 613 failures in 14,719 drive-years (4.2%). The author says he had no enterprise data older than two years. This is the post Olds criticizes. — [Backblaze](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/enterprise-drive-reliability/) (figures taken from search snippets; I did not fetch the full page)
- **[community / analyst blog] [mixed]** David Rosenthal (DSHR blog), "Storage Failures in the Field," 31 Oct 2017. Re-reading later Backblaze data, he reports AFRs of 1.1% for consumer and 1.2% for enterprise disks and argues they share most of their technology. — [DSHR blog](https://blog.dshr.org/2017/10/storage-failures-in-field.html) (from search snippet)
- **[journalism] [against: vendor objection]** HEXUS reports that in 2015 Seagate disputed Backblaze's results as inconsistent with data from its other customers and OEMs, and objected to consumer drives being used in enterprise settings. HEXUS's own view: the results need caution, but all drives face the same conditions, so comparisons within the fleet are apples-to-apples. — [HEXUS](https://hexus.net/qacoag) (search snippet; original Seagate statement not found)
- **[journalism] [mixed]** The ST3000DM001 class action (N.D. Cal., filed 2016) "primarily cited reliability data provided by Backblaze." Backblaze's data was treated as evidence in court, but no ruling on its validity was found. — [DatacenterDynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/seagate-is-being-sued-over-faulty-hard-drives/). Tom's Hardware is reported (via Wikipedia) as noting that the ST3000DM001 lacked a rotational-vibration sensor, which matters in dense multi-drive chassis. — [Wikipedia: ST3000DM001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ST3000DM001) (secondary)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [mixed]** Backblaze's own reporting thresholds admit that small per-model samples are a problem. Models are included only above 100 drives / 10,000 drive-days (quarterly), 250 / 50,000 (annual) and 500 / 100,000 (lifetime). — [Backblaze Q1 2026](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q1-2026/); [Backblaze research post, Aug 2026](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/?p=113304)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [in favor: richer context now published]** Q2 2023 added `vault_id`, `pod_id` and `is_legacy_format`. Q3 2023 added `datacenter` (ams5, iad1, phx1, sac0, sac2), `cluster_id` and `pod_slot_num` (physical slot in the server). Caveats from the same post: `datacenter` is sometimes blank "when the system is busy"; `cluster_id` "is not always accurate"; sac0's higher AFR may be confounded by older drives; the per-site comparison is "not yet conclusive." 354 drives (about 0.0013%) exceeded the vendor's maximum temperature for at least one day. — [Backblaze Q3 2023](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q3-2023/); [Backblaze Q2 2023](https://backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q2-2023/)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [mixed]** Backblaze itself says the data is a good test in some ways but "in others, it's a bit deceptive," for example because home users often lack drive-health alerts. — [Backblaze, "How an Open Dataset Powers Academic and AI/ML Research," 13 Aug 2026](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/?p=113304)
- **[peer-reviewed] [in favor: comparable field-scale studies exist]** Schroeder and Gibson (FAST '07), about 100,000 disks at HPC and internet-services sites: annual replacement rates typically above 1%, often 2 to 4%, up to 13% on some systems. SCSI, FC and SATA drives had similar replacement rates, "suggesting that operating conditions may matter more" than drive class. This partly answers the "consumer vs enterprise" objection. — [USENIX FAST '07](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast-07/disk-failures-real-world-what-does-mttf-1000000-hours-mean-you)
- **[peer-reviewed] [in favor: model-level differences are real and Backblaze data supports their study]** Siemroth and Park, "Are There Manufacturer Differences in Hard-Drive Reliability?," *IEEE Transactions on Cloud Computing*, uses Backblaze data to compare four manufacturers. — listed in [Backblaze research post](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/?p=113304) (I did not read the paper)

### Inferences
- For *this* project, consumer vs enterprise matters less than two things: (a) the fleet is one archival workload (write-once, read-rarely, few power cycles), so SMART attributes driven by workload (power cycles, load cycles, seek errors) have little variance; (b) model mix comes from purchasing. Because the project uses the top-30 model identity as a feature, "SMART helps beyond model/vendor" is a statement about *Backblaze's* model mix.
- The 2014 to 2015 critiques were about headline AFR comparisons, not about SMART-based prediction. They are relevant here mainly through the per-model sample-size concern and the skew from a few bad models.
- The new site/cluster/slot columns only exist from 2023, so the 2016 to 2025 window cannot control for environment over most of its length.

### Gaps
- Henry Newman's original Enterprise Storage Forum critique (2014) could not be retrieved; it is known only through The Register and Slashdot.
- No recent (2022 to 2026) peer-reviewed critique found that is specifically about Backblaze representativeness. The critiques found are 2014 to 2017 journalism and blogs.
- No primary Seagate statement found; only HEXUS's account.
- No published Backblaze description of workload (read/write mix, utilization) per vault that would let one compare it with other fleets.

## Q3. Known data-quality issues in the dataset

### Takeaway
Backblaze documents the schema changing over time, unreliable new metadata fields, missing first-day failures, false-positive failures at quarter boundaries, and its exclusion rules. Vendor-specific SMART raw encodings (Seagate's packed 48-bit values for attributes 1, 7 and 195) are well documented by the community but not by Backblaze. I found no authoritative source for missing days, duplicate rows or model-name inconsistencies; any such claims should come from the project's own checks.

### Cited Findings
- **[primary: Backblaze] [against: schema drift]** "Schema changes from quarter to quarter do occur." Backblaze publishes a CSV of schema changes from Q1 2018 onward. Per-quarter file counts differ (e.g., 90 files for 2025 Q1, 91 for 2026 Q2), and the page lists two different sizes for the same quarter (Q3 2025: 11.73 GB vs 11.32 GB) without explanation. — [Backblaze Hard Drive Test Data](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/resources/hard-drive-test-data)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [against: SMART semantics differ by vendor]** SMART 1 (Read_Error_Rate): "The raw value has different structure for different vendors"; "Since the manufacturers don't tell us what their attribute values could be, this SMART stat is not very useful." — [Backblaze, 2014](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/hard-drive-smart-stats/). "Not all drive manufacturers report both attributes" (197/198). — [Backblaze, 2016](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/what-smart-stats-indicate-hard-drive-failures/)
- **[community] [against: encoding]** Seagate packs several counters into one 48-bit raw value for attributes 1 and 7 (and 195), so raw numbers look huge. Decoding separates the error count (upper bits) from the operation count, and normalized values drift with workload. — [DiskTuna, "Big scary raw SMART values"](https://www.disktuna.com/big-scary-raw-s-m-a-r-t-values-arent-always-bad-news/); [ktz blog, "Reading obfuscated Seagate SMART stats with smartctl"](https://blog.ktz.me/reading-obfuscatedseagate-smart-stats-with-smartctl/); [snapraid-daemon issue #72](https://github.com/amadvance/snapraid-daemon/issues/72). Not peer-reviewed; consistent with Backblaze's 2014 remark about SMART 1.
- **[primary: Backblaze] [against: missing early failures]** Day-one failures are absent from the data. The quarter-end lookback creates "a handful" of false-positive failures. — [Backblaze Q1 2026](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q1-2026/)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [against: metadata quality]** `datacenter` is sometimes blank; `cluster_id` "is not always accurate." — [Backblaze Q3 2023](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q3-2023/)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [mixed]** For drives already running when collection began (10 Apr 2013), Backblaze estimated drive-days from SMART 9 (power-on hours), and failing drives sometimes reported bad values. Earlier analyses removed boot drives and drives with missing or implausible values. — [Backblaze, "10 Stories from 10 Years of Drive Stats"](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/10-stories-from-10-years-of-drive-stats-data/) (via search summary; not fetched in full)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [mixed]** In Q1 2026 alone, Backblaze excluded 3,907 boot drives and 492 hard drives from analysis. Boot drives (SSDs since around 2018) are in the raw data and must be removed, as this project does. — [Backblaze Q1 2026](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q1-2026/)
- **[preprint / peer-reviewed] [against: label noise and missingness]** A 2026 ACM survey ("Failure Prediction in Cloud-based Storage Systems: A Survey," DOI 10.1145/3794843) reportedly notes Backblaze labels are noisy, root causes are not documented, class imbalance is high and logs are inconsistent. It also reports missing data in both Backblaze and Alibaba data, with Alibaba's rate higher. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3794843) (paywalled, HTTP 403; claims taken from search-engine snippets only, so verify before citing)
- **[community] [against: evaluation practice]** A peer-reviewed study of imbalanced disk-failure prediction warns that "models with overly optimistic results due to data leakage provide a biased estimate of the true performance." Practitioner projects on Backblaze data report daily positive prevalence of about 0.04%, and ROC-AUC 0.84 alongside PR-AUC 0.05. — [ScienceDirect, *Machine Learning with Applications* 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666827022000585); [GitHub example](https://github.com/ChetanYarlagadda/hard-drive-failure-prediction) (community)

### Inferences
- Raw SMART 1, 7 and 195 should not be compared across vendors without decoding Seagate's packed format. With vendor and model as features, tree models can partly absorb this, but linear or pooled-scale models would be misled.
- Attribute availability varies by vendor and era. Missingness itself can encode vendor/model, which overlaps with the model/vendor features and can make SMART look more or less useful than it is.
- Because `failure=1` comes from the last day a drive was seen, quarter-boundary false positives and absent day-one failures bias the labels in opposite directions. Both are small, but they are concentrated in particular models and quarters.

### Gaps
- **No authoritative source found** for: missing calendar days in the daily files, duplicate (date, serial) rows, model-name inconsistencies (e.g., "HGST" vs "Hitachi" prefixes, trailing spaces), serials that report after a `failure=1` row, or `capacity_bytes = -1`. These are commonly mentioned in practitioner circles, but I could not find citable documentation. The project's own data checks are the best evidence here.
- The contents of Backblaze's schema-change CSV (Q1 2018 onward) were not retrieved.

## Q4. How widely is Backblaze used as a benchmark, and do studies on proprietary fleets (Google, Microsoft, Alibaba, Baidu, EMC) reach the same conclusions? Is there evidence on generalizing from Backblaze to other fleets?

### Takeaway
Backblaze is the main public benchmark: more than 227 papers have used it since 2018, and FAST '20 states that "most of the previous works" use it. Studies on proprietary fleets agree that some SMART attributes (especially reallocated and pending sectors) correlate with failure. They disagree on how far SMART alone gets. Google (2007) concluded SMART alone is "unlikely to be useful for predicting individual drive failures." Alibaba's FAST '20 study measured SMART-only models far below models that also use performance and location data. Alibaba's public SMART dataset produced competition F-scores around 0.40, far below the over-0.9 figures common in Backblaze papers. I found no peer-reviewed study that trains on Backblaze and tests on another operator's fleet.

### Cited Findings
- **[primary: Backblaze] [in favor: benchmark status]** "More than 227 papers and articles have cited Drive Stats as a primary dataset since 2018" (Google Scholar query). Examples: TFBEST (arXiv 2023), Mohapatra et al. LSTM end-of-life (IEEE 2023), Ahmed and Green II (*Neural Computing and Applications* 37, 2024), Oakley et al. (*Applied Stochastic Models in Business and Industry* 40(3), 2023), Siemroth and Park (*IEEE TCC*). — [Backblaze, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/?p=113304)
- **[peer-reviewed] [mixed]** Lu, Luo, Patel, Yao, Tiwari, Shi, "Making Disk Failure Predictions SMARTer!," USENIX FAST '20, pp. 151–167, 2020. About 380,000 HDDs, 64 sites, two months, at "one of the biggest e-commerce sites." "Most of the previous works propose prediction methods based on the publicly available Backblaze data." Figure 8 results, 10-day horizon (my reading of text extracted from the PDF): SMART-only features gave a mean F-measure of 0.46 across five models (best CNN-LSTM F = 0.58, MCC = 0.54). SMART + performance + location gave CNN-LSTM F = 0.95 and MCC = 0.95. The authors write that "SMART attributes alone may not be able to predict all disk failures," and on the hourly Baidu dataset, a failed disk's normalized SMART values "do not vary noticeably" 477 hours before failure. Portability: training on one site and testing on another makes MCC "drop significantly"; training on 62 sites and testing on unseen sites keeps MCC above 0.90 for CNN-LSTM. RF and GBDT drop more than 15% in some cases. — [USENIX page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast20/presentation/lu); [PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast20-lu.pdf)
- **[peer-reviewed] [against: SMART-only sufficiency]** Pinheiro, Weber, Barroso, "Failure Trends in a Large Disk Drive Population," USENIX FAST '07 (Google). Some SMART parameters "correlate highly with failures," but "models based on SMART parameters alone are unlikely to be useful for predicting individual drive failures." Temperature and activity levels "were much less correlated with drive failures than previously reported." — [USENIX FAST '07](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/events/fast07/tech/pinheiro.html). (The often-quoted figure that 56% of failed drives had no counts in the four strongest SMART signals, and about 36% had no signal even when all were considered, comes from the paper body; I did not re-check it in this session.)
- **[peer-reviewed] [in favor: SMART signal is real]** Ma, Douglis, Lu, Hildebrand, Chamness, Chandra, Hsu, "RAIDShield: Characterizing, Monitoring, and Proactively Protecting Against Disk Failures," USENIX FAST '15 (EMC). About one million SATA disks, six models, up to five years: the reallocated-sector count "correlates strongly with impending failures." Proactive replacement based on it is reported to eliminate 88% of triple-disk errors (vendor slides give 70% for one component). — [USENIX FAST '15](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast15/technical-sessions/presentation/ma); [SNIA SDC 2015 slides](https://www.snia.org/sites/default/files/SDC15_presentations/hw/Ao%20Ma_RaidShield_Characterizing.pdf)
- **[peer-reviewed] [mixed]** Xu et al., "Improving Service Availability of Cloud Systems by Predicting Disk Error," USENIX ATC '18, pp. 481–494 (Microsoft Azure). A cost-sensitive ranking model uses both disk-level SMART and system-level signals, evaluated on production data and deployed in Azure. This is further evidence that operators find SMART insufficient alone. — [USENIX ATC '18](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc18/presentation/xu-yong); [Microsoft Research PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2018/04/DiskErrorPrediction_cameraReady_final.pdf)
- **[peer-reviewed] [in favor: SMART predicts replacement on a non-Backblaze fleet]** Botezatu, Giurgiu, Bogojeska, Wiesmann, "Predicting Disk Replacement towards Reliable Data Centers," ACM KDD 2016, pp. 39–48, DOI 10.1145/2939672.2939699 (IBM). More than 30,000 disks from two manufacturers over 17 months; predicts the need for replacement 10–15 days ahead with up to 98% accuracy. The label is again *replacement*. The search found no evidence the data came from Backblaze (it cites Backblaze's SMART blog). — [ACM / Unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F2939672.2939699); [VideoLectures](https://videolectures.net/kdd2016_botezatu_disk_replacement/)
- **[peer-reviewed] [against: Backblaze-level scores may not transfer]** Alibaba released daily SMART logs for more than 200,000 HDDs (2017-07-31 to 2018-12-31, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) for the PAKDD 2020 AIOps competition. The organizers describe "data noises and the extreme data imbalance." One team's XGBoost pipeline reached an F-score of 39.98 for 30-day prediction. Proceedings: *Large-Scale Disk Failure Prediction*, Springer CCIS vol. 1261, 2020. — [Dataset repository](https://openi.pcl.ac.cn/Cx/Large-scale-Disk-Failure-Prediction-Dataset); [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-7749-9_12); [XJTU organizer summary](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/en/publications/summary-of-pakdd-cup-2020-from-organizers-perspective/)
- **[preprint] [mixed: cross-checks on Backblaze]** Han et al., "Robust Data Preprocessing for Machine-Learning-Based Disk Failure Prediction in Cloud Production Environments," arXiv 1912.09722 (2019), developed on a cloud operator's data. They "cross-validate our findings on the public Backblaze dataset" using its two largest models, from different vendors. This shows methods moving *to* Backblaze, not models trained on Backblaze moving to other fleets. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09722)
- **[peer-reviewed] [mixed]** "A Disk Failure Prediction Method Based on Active Semi-supervised Learning," ACM (2022), DOI 10.1145/3523699, evaluated on Backblaze and Baidu, reporting 80–85% failure detection rates at low false-alarm rates, with an Alibaba SSD dataset for generality. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3523699) (search snippet only)
- **[peer-reviewed] [mixed]** Baidu's public dataset is hourly, a single disk model, and preprocessed. It is described as closer to data-center usage but narrower than Backblaze. — per [Lu et al. FAST '20](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast20-lu.pdf) and search snippets of the [ACM survey](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3794843)

### Inferences
- The literature agrees that SMART carries *some* signal: Google, EMC, IBM, Backblaze and Alibaba all find reallocated/pending-sector-type attributes informative. It does not agree that SMART alone predicts most failures. Fleets that log more than SMART (Microsoft, Alibaba) report large gains from non-SMART features. This is the main external evidence that a Backblaze-only conclusion ("SMART helps") is likely true in direction but not in magnitude.
- High scores reported on Backblaze may partly reflect (a) the circular labels (Q1), (b) leakage from random splits, and (c) a homogeneous workload. The Alibaba competition's F of about 0.40 is a useful outside benchmark of how hard noisy production SMART data is.
- Lu et al.'s site-portability result suggests that training on one site and deploying elsewhere degrades results. Backblaze is effectively one operator, so cross-operator generalization is untested.

### Gaps
- **No peer-reviewed study found that trains on Backblaze and tests on another operator's HDD fleet** (or the reverse) with the same pipeline. This is the most important missing evidence for "general conclusions."
- Tencent field studies were not found in this pass.
- Mahdisoltani et al. (USENIX ATC '17, Google, proactive error prediction) was not retrieved.
- I could not access the full text of the 2026 ACM survey (10.1145/3794843).

## Q5. Workload and environment factors SMART does not capture, and how they limit conclusions

### Takeaway
Field studies tie failure to things SMART does not record or records poorly: workload intensity and patterns (performance counters), physical location in rack or chassis, site, age and wear-out, and correlated or bursty failures in time. Backblaze added location fields only in 2023. Google found temperature and utilization less predictive than expected, which partly offsets the environment critique. The net effect is that "does SMART help" on Backblaze is answered conditional on one workload and one environment.

### Cited Findings
- **[peer-reviewed] [against]** Lu et al. (FAST '20): "The nature of workloads running on a system often affects the failure rates." Adding performance metrics "increases our coverage in capturing the workload characteristics... beyond what SMART attributes cover." Location helps only alongside performance features (under 10% MCC gain for CNN-LSTM). — [USENIX FAST '20 PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast20-lu.pdf)
- **[peer-reviewed] [against]** Schroeder and Gibson (FAST '07): failures are not exponential or independent in time. Time between replacements shows autocorrelation and long-range dependence; wear-out begins early; and "operating conditions may matter more" than interface class. — [USENIX FAST '07](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast-07/disk-failures-real-world-what-does-mttf-1000000-hours-mean-you)
- **[peer-reviewed] [in favor: environment less dominant than feared]** Pinheiro et al. (FAST '07): temperature and activity levels were "much less correlated with drive failures than previously reported." — [USENIX FAST '07](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/events/fast07/tech/pinheiro.html)
- **[peer-reviewed] [against]** Xu et al. (ATC '18): Microsoft's production model needs system-level signals in addition to SMART. — [USENIX ATC '18](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc18/presentation/xu-yong)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [mixed]** Backblaze rarely power-cycles drives, so power cycle count "isn't very useful for predicting failure" in its fleet. This is a workload-specific limit on a SMART attribute. — [Backblaze, 2014](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/hard-drive-smart-stats/) (via search summary)
- **[primary: Backblaze] [mixed]** Location and site columns exist only from Q2/Q3 2023. Per-site AFR differences may be confounded by age (sac0 older) and are "not yet conclusive." A few drives exceeded rated temperatures. — [Backblaze Q3 2023](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q3-2023/)
- **[peer-reviewed] [mixed: time granularity]** On hourly Baidu data, a failed disk's SMART values were flat for 477 hours before failure. Backblaze's daily snapshots are coarser still. — [Lu et al. FAST '20](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast20-lu.pdf)

### Inferences
- In this project, drive model probably acts as a proxy for several things SMART does not capture: purchase cohort, deployment era, chassis generation (`is_legacy_format`), and site. Some of the "model" effect is environment, not hardware.
- Correlated failures over time (Schroeder and Gibson), plus label bursts from operations (Q1), argue for evaluating on later time periods and reporting variation between quarters, not only pooled metrics.
- The question "do SMART readings help beyond model/vendor" is well posed on Backblaze. Extending the answer to other fleets requires assuming that workload and environment effects are similar. Lu et al. and Xu et al. suggest that assumption does not hold for interactive cloud or e-commerce workloads.

### Gaps
- No Backblaze-published per-drive workload metrics (I/O rates, read/write mix) found; the public data has no performance features, so the Lu et al. comparison cannot be reproduced on it.
- No peer-reviewed study of vibration effects in Backblaze Storage Pods found; the vibration argument is anecdotal (vendor and forum statements).
